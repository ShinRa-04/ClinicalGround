## What merits attention

1. **Do not treat an open leaderboard PR as a verified score.** The striking 94.7% full-suite claim was still an unmerged PR on October 28. Its packet passed automated checks, but the public diff did not include the agent source and no maintainer review was visible. The official public board was last refreshed August 12 and did not include it. The reported 40% U2Med result was also still an open PR.
2. **Task-specific expected answers are a real submission-integrity risk.** In merged MedArise PR #7, an organizer identified answer-bearing task overlays in an earlier packet. The contributor removed them, reran, and the cleaned PA-only packet was approved at 17/25 (68%). This supports case-agnostic guidance and provenance review; it does not establish a full-suite result.
3. **Pinned versions and effective configuration matter.** Organizers specified Harbor 0.20.0, Claude Code 2.1.219, and 1,800 seconds per trial for the private evaluation described in the relevant Kaggle threads. A participant showed that a prompt-append argument was split by the shell in readable trajectories; the host reproduced it. A submission must preserve the exact configuration and launch command, and verify what arguments actually reach the process.
4. **Use the assigned workflow and terminal action.** P2P is an actual dialogue workflow: select a slot, schedule the session, then exchange context/turns. A recorded outcome alone is not a conversation. For P2P, `determination.finalize` is the final determination action; a second medical-director submission is not required. This was clarified for public benchmark versions and must be checked against the local pinned task contract before use.
5. **Keep completion, evidence provenance, and patient autonomy together.** Official analyses report many near-complete failures caused by omitted terminal actions, wrong workflow paths, or unsupported evidence. For CM, consent and assessment evidence need transcript and source provenance. The paper has a material ambiguity about hard-refusal tasks; do not turn one interpretation into a blanket care rule without resolving the applicable contract.
6. **A large context or large handbook dump is not a universal fix.** The official paper reports different handbook effects by domain and poor completion in a multi-task marathon even with a 1M-token context. Work isolation, selective retrieval, and explicit terminal reconciliation are plausible general design hypotheses, not guaranteed score improvements.

## Terms used here

- **Harness:** The agent runner and supporting control logic that presents tools, manages model calls, validates actions, and captures results.
- **Packet / submission packet:** A bundle of trial records and evidence (for example manifests, scorecards, trajectories, and provenance) submitted for review. Packet validation checks format and integrity properties; by itself it does not prove autonomous completion or clinical correctness.
- **Trajectory:** The recorded sequence of model/tool interactions during a run.
- **Simulator:** The benchmark environment that exposes applications, records, and simulated counterparties such as patients or provider/payer staff.
- **Judge:** The evaluator that checks resulting state and artifacts, and may also assess qualitative criteria. A clean structural scorecard, a judge reward, and an official competition score are distinct evidence.
- **pass@1 / pass^3 / pass@k:** pass@1 is success on a single attempt; pass^3 is success on all three required attempts; pass@k generally means at least one success among k attempts. These are not interchangeable. Best-of-five selection is not pass@1.
- **PA / UM / CM / P2P:** Prior authorization / utilization management / care management / peer-to-peer clinical review, generally between a treating clinician and a payer medical reviewer.
- **MCP / REST:** Tool/API interfaces used by the agent. A REST operation being available internally does not mean it is an allowed or exposed agent tool.

## Official benchmark and controlled baseline

**Sources:** [official simulator repository](https://github.com/actava-ai/chi-bench) · [official paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16679) · [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16679) · [benchmark methodology](https://www.actava.ai/benchmarks/chi-bench) · [official leaderboard](https://www.actava.ai/benchmarks/leaderboards) · [README submission docs](https://github.com/actava-ai/chi-bench#submit-your-agent)

The benchmark covers provider prior authorization (PA), payer utilization management (UM), and care management (CM). The paper describes 20 simulated apps, 151 REST APIs, 87 MCP tools across three domain servers, 29 workflow statuses, a 1,279-document Managed-Care Operations Handbook, and synthetic records. Other public site/README text says 21 apps or 200+ tools; the Kaggle organizer explicitly said the official count is 87 MCP tools and that 200+ was a blog error. Pin counts to a cited version rather than combining them.

Each trial runs in Docker. The official producer records results, deterministic scorecards, judge rewards, provenance, and compressed tool trajectories; public packets omit workspace scratch files. The workspace judge inspects post-run state, artifacts, tool events, and dialogue transcripts. Published score is strict binary pass@1; fractional scores are diagnostic. The paper's controlled comparison covers 30 harness/model settings with three trials per task. Best stated aggregate is Claude Code + Opus 4.6 at 28% overall pass@1. Domain bests were Codex + GPT-5.5 for PA (29.3%), Claude Code + Opus 4.6 for UM (41.3%), and Claude Code + Opus 4.7 for CM (32%). These are author-run baselines, not community submissions; strict all-three pass^3 remains low.

### Official paper findings with practical value

- **Terminal completion is a frequent failure class.** The analysis attributes 23.3% of non-passes to workflow completion: missing terminal action, wrong path, premature close, or missed evidence. Reads and preparatory writes do not prove the terminal action occurred. Persisted readiness and explicit terminal-state reconciliation are useful design targets.
- **Retrieval and reasoning errors differ.** The paper separates misreading correctly retrieved criteria from applying a criterion to the wrong chart state. More retrieval alone cannot solve policy application or action selection.
- **Tool failures can cascade.** Malformed arguments, wrong tools, and invalid references are reported causes; some harnesses turn one invalid call into a fatal trial. Schema checks, classified tool errors, bounded correction of non-side-effecting calls, and read-after-write confirmation are reasonable mechanics. A prose claim of completion cannot substitute for persisted state.
- **Long context did not solve multi-task execution.** In the 25-task marathon, leading agents completed few terminal tasks; PA agents touched cases but submitted no authorizations. Codex compacted context several times; Claude's 1M-token setup did not compact but failed similarly. This supports testing per-task isolation/checkpoints and explicit queue-to-terminal accounting, not assuming larger context is enough.
- **Handbook utility varies by domain.** Removing the handbook reduced UM performance. In PA, the handbook sometimes drove over-verification and refusal to submit despite adequate readiness. CM stayed near floor, with the paper attributing the bottleneck more to interaction/content than retrieval. Selective, task-relevant retrieval is a better hypothesis than dumping a manual everywhere.
- **MCP-to-CLI conversion had no uniform gain.** Official Codex + GPT-5.5 moved PA 29.3→28, UM 32→25.3, CM 1.3→4.0. Tool-surface changes should be tested per domain.
- **Record environment pins.** The public README/Kaggle page describes Claude Sonnet 5 simulated patients and P2P counterparts, with adaptive thinking disabled; Kaggle also gives a 1,024-token patient cap. Kaggle says private competition uses three judge votes, while development may use one if recorded. Judge and simulator settings are part of the result provenance, not the tested agent itself.

### CM dialogue and consent: useful principle and unresolved specification

The paper describes hidden-state patient personas with consent triggers, trust-damaging anti-triggers, enrollment thresholds, and topic-specific disclosure gates. Consent precedes formal assessment. Chart data is not a substitute for patient-reported interview evidence; sensitive social or behavioral fields should not be inferred from demographics or chart context. Sensitive questions require an explanation, permission, acknowledgment that the person may skip/stop, and applicable safety-limit disclosure. The taxonomy distinguishes fabricated consent without explicit transcript affirmation; coercively repeated reframing to obtain “yes”; ethical engagement without enrollment; and skipped or ungrounded assessment.

**Material ambiguity:** some case analyses frame a clear refusal as a decline/disenrollment to document. Appendix G's “interpretation B” says a hard-refuse task passes only after autonomy-first engagement and explicit affirmative enrollment, and treats disenrollment as failure. This is an evaluation-specification inconsistency. Do not infer that conversion of refusal is a general clinical goal; resolve which versioned local task contract applies.

## Practical, high-value implications

These are general design/evaluation implications drawn from the cited sources, not task-specific answers or expected outcomes.

1. **Keep each claim tied to evidence.** Separate code mechanics, packet checks, structural state verification, qualitative judge evaluation, and official competition score. Report exact model/version, runtime/data/image, prompt/config, attempt count, judge count, and score type.
2. **Treat completion as observed state.** Maintain a case-to-terminal-action checklist and confirm persisted state after consequential writes. Do not infer completion from a “ready” artifact or natural-language summary.
3. **Preserve source and interaction provenance.** Keep chart, patient utterance, external record, and future/planned information distinct. Unknown remains valid. Assessment should only record information actually elicited and permitted.
4. **Tie consent to explicit evidence and respect the applicable task contract.** Keep consent distinct from willingness to hear information; avoid coercive repeated reframing. Because the paper is internally inconsistent about hard refusal, use the versioned local contract and do not make a universal refusal-to-enrollment rule.
5. **Make tool handling robust but bounded.** Validate arguments and identifiers before dispatch; retain typed errors; only retry a known failed, non-side-effecting operation; after a lost response, determine whether a write committed before considering another write.
6. **Isolate work by case/task.** Test checkpoints, small case-indexed summaries, and queue-to-terminal accounting rather than assuming a larger context will prevent dropped cases or premature stopping.
7. **Retrieve selectively.** UM appears policy/handbook-sensitive; PA can be harmed by excess verification; CM more often suffers interaction and grounding limitations. Test scoped references by domain rather than injecting the entire handbook universally.
8. **Keep configs reproducible.** Verify the effective command-line value and runtime pins, record repaired data/runtime versions and image digest, and preserve old trials. Do not mix results across public asset repairs as though they used the same environment.
9. **Use case-agnostic rules only.** The MedArise review shows why answer-bearing overlays undermine integrity. Keep task IDs, hidden expected outcomes, and private persona material out of actor behavior rules.
10. **Treat prompt length and tool-surface changes as hypotheses.** The GAMPO study was exploratory and domain effects differed; MCP-to-CLI results also differed by domain. Use paired, well-labeled runs before claiming improvement.
