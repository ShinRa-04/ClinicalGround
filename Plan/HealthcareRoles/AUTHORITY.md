## The rule for interpreting superior decisions

A decision can supersede another decision **only within an identified domain, process and jurisdiction**. A job title or reporting relationship is insufficient. Specify: the issue, product/setting, professional qualification, delegated authority, current stage, patient/representative status and applicable review route.

The question is not simply "who is senior?" It is "whose authorized decision controls this particular issue at this stage, under which rule, subject to which review?" Cases can require several compatible permissions; one domain does not swallow all the others.

## Distinct authority domains

| Domain | What controls it | What does not automatically override it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Patient participation/consent | Patient rights and lawful representative authority within scope | A manager, service booking or payer approval |
| Clinical recommendation/order | Authorized treating professional, license and privileges | Coder, coordinator, system administrator or call-center title |
| Professional scope/delegation | Applicable law, license, competence and permitted practice arrangement | Work assignment or higher salary/title |
| Administrative benefits | Actual plan rules and delegated administrative decision authority | Treating-clinician recommendation alone |
| Clinical coverage determination | Applicable coverage criteria and properly qualified/delegated reviewer | An intake clerk's inability to resolve the request |
| Appeal/adjudication | Properly invoked review route and qualified/independent decision-maker | Internal reporting rank outside that route |
| Policy governance | Applicable rule and designated policy-approval process | An unapproved analyst draft |
| Operational management | Actual organizational mandate, staffing and process authority | It does not itself confer prescribing, consent or appeal powers |
| Information access/privacy | Applicable purpose, rights, permissions and restrictions | Technical ability to open a record |

These are a synthesis of the cited authorities, not an invented single national organizational chart. See [authority domain graph](graphs/authority-domains.svg).

## Patient and clinician

In the hospital setting, 42 CFR 482.13(b) gives patients participation and informed-decision rights, including requesting or refusing treatment. The same provision does not turn the right into an entitlement to demand medically inappropriate services. HHS representative guidance makes the extent of representative authority depend on applicable law and circumstances. Capacity, minors, emergency exceptions, guardianship conflicts and state surrogate priority are **not resolved by a generic role title**. Establish the applicable rule for the case. [A01](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-482/subpart-B/section-482.13), [A09](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/personal-representatives/index.html)

An ordering clinician's clinical instruction and a payer's coverage determination answer different questions. The payer reviewer is not made the treating doctor's supervisor. An authorization is not patient consent; it is also not a guarantee of final payment. Those boundaries are reflected in D01, D03, D10, D18 and D32. [R06](https://health.ucsd.edu/insurance-billing/paying/faq/), [A02](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-M/section-422.566)

## Medicare Advantage: two different qualification rules

| Stage | Verified scoped rule | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial anticipated partially/fully adverse medical-necessity determination | 422.566(d): physician **or another appropriate healthcare professional**, with relevant expertise, Medicare-criteria knowledge and current unrestricted professional license | A physician-only rule for every initial denial would be inaccurate; a clerk is not an appropriate substitute |
| Reconsideration of medical-necessity denial | 422.590(h): reconsiderers were not involved in the initial determination; the medical-necessity reconsidered determination is made by an appropriately expert physician | A senior initial reviewer does not become independent merely through promotion/rank |
| Remaining disputed adverse MA issues | Independent CMS-contracted entity under 422.592, with scoped forwarding requirements in 422.590 | This is the MA IRE route, not generic commercial external review |
| Implementation of reversal | 422.618 specifies effectuation by decision type and includes further-review conditions/exceptions | A favorable review and actual implementation are separate milestones |

Sources: [A02](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-M/section-422.566), [A03](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-M/section-422.590), [A04](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-M/section-422.592), [A16](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-M/section-422.618).

These statements are for the specified MA route. The integrated-plan exception described in 422.566(a), decision category, other products and actual notices must be checked. The document does not silently apply the same rules to commercial insurance, Medicaid, Part D, Original Medicare or every expedited process.

CMS describes successive MA appeal levels: plan reconsideration, independent entity, adjudication, Council and district court. Eligibility, jurisdiction, filing rules and any amount-in-controversy requirements remain conditions; the ladder is not an automatic entitlement. A16 also contains an effectuation exception when the plan seeks Council review. [A11](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/appeals-grievances/managed-care-appeals-grievances/appeals-overview)

See [MA review hierarchy](graphs/ma-appeals.svg). The forward arrows mean a permitted next review, not that the later forum is the employer of the earlier reviewer.

## Commercial external review

HealthCare.gov describes an applicable external-review process in which an insurer must accept the external outcome. The actual state/federal route, plan and eligible issue must first be identified. Do not route an MA dispute to a commercial IRO because both are called independent review. Do not assume a peer discussion is an appeal or preserves an appeal deadline. [A12](https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/external-review/), [R15](https://www.uhcprovider.com/en/claims-payments-billing/appeals.html)

See [commercial review graph](graphs/commercial-appeals.svg). Precise deadlines and filing instructions must come from the actual process/notice; this reference is not a filing calculator.

The pathway need not be strictly sequential. HealthCare.gov describes eligible urgent external review requested at the same time as internal appeal, without completing all internal steps. Check the applicable plan, issue and external-review eligibility; the CMS overview specifically describes the applicable ACA/non-grandfathered context. [V19](https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/internal-appeals/), [R17](https://www.cms.gov/cciio/programs-and-initiatives/consumer-support-and-information/csg-ext-appeals-facts)

## Professional supervision and administrative rank

NCSBN directs users to state Nurse Practice Acts and a scope decision framework. BLS descriptions also identify variation for NP, PA, LPN and other roles. An employer's instruction is not evidence that the person may perform a prohibited clinical task. Nursing, pharmacy, therapy and social-work professional responsibility cannot be replaced by an invented "doctor > nurse > everyone" organizational ladder. [A13](https://ncsbn.org/nursing-regulation/practice/decision-making-framework.page), [A14](https://ncsbn.org/policy/npa.page)

The [reporting examples](graphs/reporting-examples.svg) show only described relationships from UCSD R14 and Partnership R32. The latter explicitly differentiates RN, LVN, social-work and nonclinical supervisory functions. Those descriptions do not prove the hierarchy of another organization or identical professional powers for all supervisors.

R32 describes associate-director direction of managers and supervisors, and manager/supervisor collaboration in oversight. The graph now distinguishes that collaboration with a dashed edge; it does not invent a manager-to-supervisor direct reporting chain. In the AZPC program example, limited delegated nonclinical approvals/benefit-only decisions coexist with licensed review. R13 also contains wider professional categories alongside medical-director final-responsibility language; exact case signoff remains ambiguous without further delegation/product clarification. That ambiguity does not authorize nonclinical medical-necessity denials. See D11 and U01–U08.

## Policy, systems and records

For MA, 422.137 identifies UM policy-governance responsibilities; 422.101 constrains coverage criteria. Committee approval is distinct from an individual appeal. A drafter proposes policy; the designated process approves it. A system rule is not necessarily the controlling policy version. [A05](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-C/section-422.137), [A17](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-C/section-422.101)

Hospital record entries and orders have authentication requirements in 482.24. Coders, CDI, HIM and EHR personnel have different contributions; technical or documentary access does not authorize fabrication of the clinical facts. [A15](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-482/subpart-C/section-482.24)

HHS lists exceptions to the HIPAA minimum-necessary standard, including specified provider treatment disclosures/requests. That does not erase other privacy duties or make every data exchange unrestricted. Nor is it correct to require written patient authorization for every treatment exchange. Check purpose, recipient and any specially protected material. [A10](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html)

Applicable Part 2 records add further distinctions: the HHS fact sheet describes a future treatment/payment/operations consent option and separate consent treatment for specified SUD counseling notes. The minimum-necessary exception alone therefore cannot settle every sharing decision. [V15](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/regulatory-initiatives/fact-sheet-42-cfr-part-2-final-rule/index.html)

## Required local authority record

Before treating this desk reference as an institution-specific operating model, record:

1. Institution, service and insurance product; governing state and professional jurisdiction.
2. Actual license, privileges, competence and applicable practice/delegation arrangement.
3. Relevant policy/version and evidence of approval/effective scope.
4. Actual organogram and assigned clinical versus administrative supervisor.
5. Patient decision-making/representation facts and appointment limits.
6. Current case stage, decision type, notice and review route.
7. Signatory/reviewer independence, expertise and conflict requirements.
8. Data-sharing purpose, permission and relevant restrictions.
9. What counts as receipt, substantive decision, implementation and completed service.

Until these facts are supplied, the exact local permission is **not established**, not "probably allowed." The profiles state useful boundaries without inventing those facts.
